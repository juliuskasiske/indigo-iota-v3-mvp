Start here: what is this product?

Read this section first if you have not seen the project before. It uses no technical vocabulary. Everything after it builds on the picture drawn here.

The problem it solves

Management consultants are hired to find out why a company is underperforming and what to do about it. A large part of that job is unglamorous. Before anyone can give advice, somebody has to read an enormous quantity of the company's material — emails, reports, spreadsheets, meeting notes — and work out what is actually going on. That reading takes weeks, and it is expensive, because senior people do it.

Worse, the understanding does not survive. It lives in the heads of whoever did the reading. When they move to another project it leaves with them, and the next team starts from nothing.

What Indigo Iota does about it

Iota does that reading continuously and automatically, and keeps what it learns. It does two distinct jobs. The difference between them is the single most useful thing to understand about the product.

Job one: build a memory

Connect a company's mailbox and shared documents. Iota reads what arrives and turns it into organised knowledge: a page for each person, each company, each deal, and a map of how they connect.

You can then ask questions in ordinary English and get an answer that cites the email or document it came from.

Chapters 1 to 9.

Job two: form a diagnosis

Tell it what the company is trying to achieve — say, double revenue within two years. Iota works through its memory and builds an argument about where the money is leaking.

The result is a tree you can read and disagree with: the goal at the top, possible explanations beneath it, specific things to do at the bottom.

Chapter 10, which is new.

The first job is a very good librarian. The second is a consultant who has read everything the librarian filed. The second is only possible because the first exists.

The promise the design is built around

This system reads a company's private correspondence. That is a serious thing to be trusted with, and three decisions in the architecture exist because of it.

Each customer's knowledge lives in its own separate database. Not one shared table with a customer column — a genuinely separate database. Two customers' data cannot come back from the same query, even by mistake.

Not everything gets read. Before an email reaches the memory it passes a filter that sorts it into one of four buckets, and only one bucket is kept. Legal advice, individual pay and performance, medical matters and personal mail are meant to be excluded. A rejected email leaves a record that it was rejected, but its content is never stored.

The processing stays in Europe. The service that reads text is hosted in the EU, and the step that turns text into a searchable form runs on our own machines, so that step sends nothing outside at all.

What it costs to run, and why that is visible

Reading text with an AI model costs money per page. So every call is recorded: which model, how much text went in and out, what it cost at the price in force that day, and which customer it belonged to. A workspace is funded up front, and the software stops when the money runs out. It cannot quietly run up a bill.

If you read one more thing, read Chapter 1. It defines every technical word used anywhere else in this guide — server, database, embedding, container, token, agent — with analogies and no assumed knowledge.

The shape of the repository

At the very top, the project is four folders. Almost everything else lives inside them.

indigo-iota-v3-mvp/
├── backend/     the "engine": Python (FastAPI) + the databases. Does the real work.
├── frontend/    the "face": the Next.js website you click around in.
├── infra/       the "plumbing": Docker recipes to run it locally and in production.
└── docs/        notes & this explainer (never shipped to customers).
Think of a restaurant. frontend is the dining room you see and touch. backend is the kitchen that actually cooks. infra is the building, wiring, and gas lines that let the kitchen run. docs is the staff binder in the back office.
